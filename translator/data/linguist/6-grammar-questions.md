# Grammar questions

Each question is something the lesson book leaves open. Please answer in everyday
(spoken) Buxori and say if formal or written usage differs. Use the book's Latin
spelling (ü, ' for ayin). "Translator" is what the site outputs today.

## A. "To be" with a place

1. "I am here." Translator: `Man insun`. Is that right, or `Man insunam`, or `Man insun hastam`?
2. "You are at home." (to one person, informal) Translator: `Tu ba xona`. Correct form, or `Tu ba xonayi`?
3. "We are in Israel." Translator: `Mo ba Isroel`. Correct form?
4. "He is not here." Translator: `Ü insun nest`. Correct?
5. Is `xonaba` or `ba xona` the everyday way to say "at home / to the house"? Are both fine? Any difference between "at" and "to"?

## B. Past tense, he/she

6. The book gives two endings for "he/she went": `raftak` (Regular) and `raft` (Literal). Which do people actually say? Is `-ak` used with every verb?
7. "He was a doctor." Translator: `Ü düxtur budak`. Or `Ü düxtur bud`?

## C. "If"

8. The book prints `Agar bovar namekuned, …` (with me-) and `Agar kuchukat lelahota lesad, …` (without me-). Which form follows `agar`? Please translate:
   - "If you go, I go."  (Translator: `Agar tu meri, man merum`)
   - "If he comes, I will go."  (Translator: `Agar ü mebiyo, man merum`)
   - "If I had money, I would buy a house."
   - "If it rains, I don't go."

## D. Present and future

9. The book uses one form for both ("merum" = I go / I will go). Is there a way to make the future explicit ("I will go tomorrow")? Please translate "I will go tomorrow" and "I am going to buy a book."

## E. "The man who…"

10. Please translate (the book has one example: `Marde, ki az xona buromad, padari man bud`):
    - "The man who came is my father."
    - "The book that I read is good."
    - "The woman whom I saw is my teacher."
    - "The house where I live is big."
    - Does the noun always take `-e` before `ki`? Also with plurals ("the men who came")?

## F. Possession

11. "my brothers": the book has one example, `Akahoyam`. Translator builds `barodarhoyam`. Correct? Please give "my books", "your (informal) books", "our friends".
12. "my big house": `xoni kaloni man` or `xoni kalonam`, or both?
13. "David's book" and "the teacher's house": please translate.

## G. Objects

14. When is the object marker `-a / -ya` required? Please translate both:
    - "I read a book."  /  "I read the book."
    - "I see him."  (Translator: `Man üya mebinam`)
15. "Give me the book." Translator: `Ba man kitova te!` (informal), `Ba man kitova ted!` (polite). Correct? Where does "me" go?

## H. Word order

16. Where do time words go? Translator: `Man aknun ba Isroel zindagi mekunam` ("I live in Israel now"). Natural?
17. `qati` ("with"): always after the noun (`barodaram qati`)? Is `bo` used in speech?
18. Questions without a question word: "Do you have a book?" Translator: `Tu kitov dori?`. Is there a question particle (`-mi`)? When is it used?

## I. Continuous tenses

19. The book gives `rafsodim` (Regular) and `rafta istodayam` (Literal) for "I am going". Which is everyday speech? Is `-sod-` used with every verb?
20. "I was reading when he came." Please translate.

## J. Commands and requests

21. "Come!" to one person / politely / to several: the book gives `biyo`. What are the polite and plural forms? Translator: `bed` (polite), `beton` (plural).
22. "Don't go!" (informal, polite). "Let's go." "Please sit."

## K. Can / must / want

23. Please confirm or correct:
    - "I can go."  Translator: `Man rafta metavonam`
    - "I must go."  Translator: `Man boyad ravam`
    - "I want to go."  Translator: `Man raftani hastam`
    - "I don't want to eat."

## L. Comparison

24. "bigger", "the biggest", "A goose is bigger than a pigeon" (book: `Qoz az kaftar kalon hast`). Is `-tar` used in speech (`kalontar`)?

## N. Where the book and a Tajik-based draft disagree

A draft of answers built from Tajik (not from a speaker) disagrees with the lesson book on these points. The translator follows the book. Please say which is right for Buxori.

26. Does the past ending `-ak` (`raftak`, `dodak`) simply mean "he/she went / gave", as the book's "Regular form" says? Or does it add "apparently / I am told", with plain `raft` as the neutral past? Please translate "He went home yesterday" (you saw it) and "They say he went home".
27. The book prints `Man guftani hastam` = "I want to say". Does `-ani hastam` mean "want to", or "about to / intend to"? How would you say "I want to go" and "I am about to go"?
28. The book glosses `vayho` as "these". Is it "these" or "those"? What are "these books" and "those books"?
29. The book glosses `garchande` as "through; in spite of". Is it a preposition with that meaning, or the conjunction "although"? Please give a sentence.
30. The book gives the present stem of `Gazidan` (to sting) as `-gazid-`, of `Püshondan` (to dress someone) as `-püsh-`, and of `Shumurdan` (to count) as `-shumur-`. What are "it stings", "I dress him", "I count"?
31. "There is a book on the table", "There is no time", "Is there water?": the book prints no pattern. How are these said?
32. "Nobody came", "I saw nothing", "I never go": the book has only `hej` with a negative verb (`Hej gham nabined!`). Are `hej kas` and `hej chiz` used?
33. "What time is it?", "How long?", "What a beautiful day!": please translate.
