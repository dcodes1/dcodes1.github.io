# Buxori translator brief: answers to Part 1 (grammar questions) and Part 11

**Read this first.** These answers were not written by a native speaker. They are reconstructed from modern Tajik (especially colloquial Northern Tajik, the Bukhara and Samarkand dialects that Buxori is closest to) and from Farsi, then fitted to the lesson book's own forms and spelling. They are an informed draft, not speaker data. Every row in the attached spreadsheets has an extra `AI_CONFIDENCE` column (high / medium / low).

- **High:** the form is printed in the book, or is standard across Tajik and Farsi.
- **Medium:** standard colloquial Tajik that very likely holds for Buxori.
- **Low:** an educated guess.

The site says entries "you supply are labelled … as additions from a speaker". Please do not import these under that label. Label them "AI draft (Tajik-derived), unverified" until a speaker confirms them.

Spelling follows the book: x = kh, gh, q, ch, sh, j, ü, ' = ayin, and a final -b after a vowel becomes -v (kitov, xov, javov).

## A. "To be" with a place

**1. "I am here."** Use **Man insunam**. Buxori adds the copula as a suffix (-am, -i, zero, -em, -ed, -and), just as the book prints *Man xalfayam* and *Man düxturam*. Bare *Man insun* is understood but clipped. *Man insun hastam* is emphatic ("I *am* here, I'm present"). *(medium)*

**2. "You are at home."** Use **Tu xonayi** or **Tu ba xonayi**. The 2nd-person-singular copula is -i, or -yi after a vowel. *(medium)*

**3. "We are in Israel."** Use **Mo Isroelem** or **Mo ba Isroelem**. *(medium)*

**4. "He is not here."** **Ü insun nest.** ✓ *(high)*

**5. xonaba vs ba xona.** In Tajik speech the noun often stands bare for both "at home" and "to home": *xona raftam*, *xona istodam*. *ba xona* is the explicit form and is always safe. I can't confirm postposed *xonaba* from Tajik. If the book prints it, treat it as a Buxori colloquialism. Tajik has no consistent at/to distinction here, and context decides. *(low)*

## B. Past tense, he/she

**6. raftak vs raft.** This is the most important open question. In Tajik, the plain past *raft* is the neutral, witnessed past. Forms like *raftak*, *raftagi* and *rafta-ast* are evidential: "he apparently went / I'm told he went / turns out he went". If Buxori works like Tajik, the translator is wrong to use *-ak* as the default. Use *raft* for ordinary narration and *raftak* only for hearsay or surprise.

The book calls *raftak* the "Regular" form, though, so Buxori may have generalized it. **A speaker must settle this.** The translator's current choice may be right only if *-ak* has lost its evidential meaning. Wherever the sheets say "dod / firistod / omad", I chose the plain past for this reason. *(low–medium)*

**7. "He was a doctor."** Use **Ü düxtur bud** (neutral). *Ü düxtur budak* means "he turned out to be / was reportedly a doctor". *(medium)*

## C. "If"

**8.** In Tajik, a real condition takes the subjunctive (no me-) after *agar*. An unreal (counterfactual) condition takes the me- + past form in both halves. Speech also allows the me- present after *agar*, so the book's two examples are both possible.

- "If you go, I go." **Agar tu ravi, man ham merum.** (informal: *Agar tu meri, …* is also heard) *(medium)*
- "If he comes, I will go." **Agar ü biyoyad, man merum.** (spoken *Agar ü biyo, …*) *(medium)*
- "If I had money, I would buy a house." **Agar man pul (shohel) medoshtam, yak xona mexaridam.** *(medium)*
- "If it rains, I don't go." **Agar boron borad, man namerum.** *(medium)*

## D. Present and future

**9.** The me- present serves as the future, and a time word makes the future explicit.

- "I will go tomorrow." **Man pagah merum.** *(high)*
- "I am going to buy a book." **Man kitov xaridani hastam.** The *-ani hastam* construction means "I am about to / intend to". This matters for Q23 below. *(medium)*

## E. "The man who…"

**10.**

- "The man who came is my father." **Marde, ki omad, padaram.** (or *padari man*) *(medium)*
- "The book that I read is good." **Kitove, ki man xondam, naghz.** *(medium)*
- "The woman whom I saw is my teacher." **Zane, ki man didam, xalfayi man.** *(medium)*
- "The house where I live is big." **Xonae, ki man (dar on) zindagi mekunam, kalon.** *(medium)*

The -e is normal before *ki* in Tajik but is often dropped in fast speech (*mard ki omad*). Plurals take it too: **mardhoe, ki omadand**. *(medium)*

## F. Possession

**11.** *barodarhoyam* ✓. The plural *-ho* plus *-yam* after a vowel matches the book's *Akahoyam*.

- "my books" = **kitovhoyam**
- "your (informal) books" = **kitovhoyat**
- "our friends" = **hoverhoyemon** (or *hoverhoyi mo*, which is safest)

*(medium; I'm unsure of the 1st-person-plural suffix. Tajik has -amon, and Buxori may have -emon, by analogy with the book's -eton.)*

**12.** Both are fine: **xoni kaloni man** and **xoni kalonam**. The suffix form is more colloquial. *(medium)*

**13.** "David's book" = **kitovi David**. "the teacher's house" = **xoni xalfa**. *(high)*

## G. Objects

**14.** The object marker -a / -ya is required when the object is definite or specific (named, a pronoun, "the …", "this …"). It is absent when the object is indefinite or generic.

- "I read a book." **Man kitov xondam / mexonam.**
- "I read the book." **Man kitova xondam / mexonam.**
- "I see him." **Man üya mebinam.** ✓ (pronoun objects always take -a/-ya: *mana, tuya, üya, moya*)

*(high)*

**15.** **Kitova ba man te!** (informal) and **Kitova ba man ted! / tiyed!** (polite). The translator's version, *Ba man kitova te*, is also acceptable. In Tajik, "me" often comes as a bare object form: **Kitova mana te!** *(medium)*

## H. Word order

**16.** *Man aknun ba Isroel zindagi mekunam* is natural. Time words go after the subject or at the very start. *ba* is optional with place names. *(medium)*

**17.** *qati* always follows its noun with no izafat: **barodaram qati**, **man qati**. *tuyi qati* is wrong; use *tu qati*. *bo* is the literary preposition placed before the noun, and it is also heard in speech, especially from educated speakers. *(medium)*

**18.** *Tu kitov dori?* ✓. Rising intonation is enough. Tajik speech also uses the particle **-mi** (*Kitov dori-mi?*), which sounds a bit insistent, roughly "do you or don't you?". Use intonation as the default. *(medium)*

## I. Continuous tenses

**19.** The contracted *-sod-* form (*rafsodim*, *xonsodim*, *kor karsodim*) is everyday speech and works with any verb that names an ongoing action. It is not used with *budan*, *doshtan*, *tavonistan* or *donistan*. *rafta istodayam* is the full literary form. *(medium)*

**20.** **Kay-ki ü omad, man kitov xonsoda budam.** Alternatively: *Man kitov xonsoda budam, ki ü omad.* *(medium)*

## J. Commands and requests

**21.** **biyo** (one person, informal). **bed** (polite and plural; the book prints it, and it is a contraction of *biyoed*). I can't confirm *beton*. The -eton ending is the 2nd-person-plural possessive (biveton), so *beton* looks like a construction by analogy. Treat it as unverified. *(medium)*

**22.**

- "Don't go!" **Narav!** (book: *narov*) (informal). **Narved! / Naraved!** (polite)
- "Let's go." **Raftem!** Colloquial Tajik uses the past form as the hortative. **Ravem!** is the textbook form.
- "Please sit." **Marhamat, shined.**

*(medium)*

## K. Can / must / want

**23.**

- "I can go." **Man rafta metavonam** ✓ *(high)*
- "I must go." **Man boyad ravam** ✓ *(high)*
- "I want to go." **Man raftan mexoham.** ✗ *raftani hastam* means "I'm about to go / I intend to go", not "want". *(medium)*
- "I don't want to eat." **Man xürdan namexoham.** For "I don't feel like eating": **Xürdagim nameyad.** *(medium)*

## L. Comparison

**24.** *-tar* is used in speech:

- "bigger" = **kalontar**
- "the biggest" = **az hama kalon** (everyday; *kalontarin* is literary)
- "A goose is bigger than a pigeon." **Qoz az kaftar kalontar.** Without -tar, the book's *az kaftar kalon* is also good colloquial Tajik.

*(medium)*

## M. Patterns the translator gets wrong

**25.** Recurring errors I found while filling worksheets 3 and 4:

1. **"want" mapped to *-ani hastam*.** That construction means "about to / intend to". Use *X-an mexoham* (raftan, xürdan, xaridan).
2. **Default 3rd-person past *-ak*.** This is possibly evidential. Use the plain past (*raft, dod, omad*) until a speaker settles Q6. *-ak* stays correct for "it is said / apparently" (*Shunidam, ki ü shavhar kardak*).
3. **Missing copula on 1st and 2nd person.** Rows like *Man ba xona* and *Man insun* need *-am / -i / -em / -ed*. 3rd person singular may stay bare (*Ü düxtur*).
4. **English "there is / there are" rendered with *unsun* (there).** Existence is *hast / nest*: *Vaqt nest*, *Maim hast?*
5. **Negatives built from *ne* plus a positive verb.** Buxori, like Tajik, uses *hej* + a negative verb: *hej kas naomad*, *hej chiz nadidam*, *hej vaqt namebiyo*.
6. **Wrong time prepositions.** "for ten years" is *dah sol (boz)*, not *baroyi*. "on Saturday" is *shabot / rüzi shabot*, not *boli*. "in two days" is *du rüz ba'd*, not *ba duta rüz*.
7. **Wrong question calques.** *Vaqt chi?* should be *So'at chand?*. *Kühna chixel?* should be *Chand sola?*. *Tu chi?* should be *Tu chi kor mekuni?*. *Daroz chixel?* should be *Chand vaqt?*.
8. **izafat put before postpositions.** *tuyi qati* should be *tu qati*.
9. **Exclamations built as questions.** *Rüzi xushrü chi?* should be *Chi rüzi xushrü!*.
10. **"these" given as *vayho*.** *vayho* means "those". "these" is *inho*.
11. **"through" and "in spite of" given as *garchande*.** *garchande* means "although" and is a conjunction.
12. **Present stems built wrongly for -idan and -ondan verbs.** *Gazidan* gives *megazam*, not *megazidam*. *Püshondan* gives *mepüshonam*, not *mepüsham*. *Shumurdan* gives *meshumoram*.
13. ***doshtan* given the me- prefix.** It takes none: *doram, nadoram*.

## Part 11: Anything else

- **Two layers of loanwords.** Everyday Buxori mixes a Tajik base with Hebrew words for religious and family life (*maim, bosor, hover, Shabot, Mazoltüv, Lahayim, parnoso, nashomo*) and Russian words for modern life (*moshin, telefon, kvartira, poezd, xolodilnik, prosent*). For modern vocabulary in Part 8, Tajik words are given first and Russian loans second. Older Bukharan Jews in Israel and New York often prefer the Russian word, and younger speakers often switch to Hebrew. A speaker should mark which one they actually say.
- **Months and weekdays.** The book gives Hebrew month names (*Nison, Iyar …*) beside Russian ones. In the word-choice sheet I set Russian as the everyday default for civil dates and Hebrew for religious ones. Please check this: some families use Hebrew months for everything.
- **The counting suffix -ta.** It is used with numbers before nouns in speech (*duta barodar, seta kitov*). It is optional with *kas* and with measure words (*du kilo, nim so'at, se sol*).
- **Spoken reductions worth supporting as alternative forms:** 3rd person singular present -ad dropped to -a (*mekuna, mexona*); *mebiyo* for *mebiyoyad*; *megüm* for *megüyam*; *meyad* for *meoyad*; *shar* for *shahr*.
- **Respect forms.** *Shon* is a respectful he/she/they. The suffix *-jon* turns names and kinship words into terms of endearment (*Bivijon, David-jon*). Elders take the plural verb (*Chitüyed?*).
- **Dialect.** Where Bukhara and Samarkand Tajik differ, I followed Bukhara.
- **Proper names and fragments in the 1,000-word list** (chicago, obama, "to trump", "to harry", "to sub", …) are tagged "(proper name / not vocabulary)". Consider removing them from the frequency list.
